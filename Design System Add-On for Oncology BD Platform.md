# 126. VISUAL DESIGN DIRECTION — CRITICAL

The visual design of this product is extremely important.

Do NOT make this look like a generic AI dashboard, generic Salesforce clone, generic startup admin template, or a collection of brightly colored cards.

The product should feel like a premium, modern life-sciences intelligence platform built for senior biopharma professionals.

Use the visual sensibilities of:

- Eli Lilly's modern digital presence: sophisticated editorial typography, strong hierarchy, generous whitespace, premium life-sciences feel, and confident use of large type.
- Viedoc: extremely clean clinical-software interface, structured information architecture, intuitive navigation, restrained UI, and an ability to present complex clinical information without making the interface feel overwhelming.
- Veza Digital's best B2B/SaaS design work: modern composition, strong typography, polished layouts, subtle interaction design, sophisticated use of gradients and visual depth, and high-end technology-company presentation.

These references are INSPIRATION ONLY.

Do not copy their layouts, branding, proprietary assets, or visual identity.

Create an original design system for this product.

The desired feeling is:

CLINICAL INTELLIGENCE
×
PREMIUM BIOTECH
×
MODERN ENTERPRISE SOFTWARE
×
AI-NATIVE WORKFLOW

The interface should feel appropriate for someone working at Pfizer, Novartis, AstraZeneca, Roche, Lilly, Gilead, Genentech, or a high-growth precision-oncology company.

It should feel credible enough to show a VP of Business Development or Head of Translational Medicine.

# 127. BRAND COLOR DIRECTION

Use DEEP PURPLE as the defining brand color.

The purple should feel sophisticated, scientific, premium, and slightly futuristic — NOT playful, neon, childish, or overly saturated.

Preferred visual family:

Deep aubergine
Dark plum
Black-purple
Muted violet
Soft lavender accents

Conceptually:

Primary background / brand:
very deep purple-black

Primary accent:
rich deep violet

Secondary accent:
muted lavender

Light accent:
extremely pale lavender / off-white

Neutral surfaces:
white
warm off-white
very light gray
charcoal

Use purple selectively.

Do NOT make every surface purple.

The strongest design should primarily use neutral surfaces with deep-purple navigation, accents, selected states, data visualization highlights, important signals, and subtle gradients.

Avoid excessive rainbow coloring.

Color should communicate meaning.

For example:

High Priority
strong but sophisticated emphasis

Medium Priority
muted emphasis

Low Priority
neutral

Positive Response
green

Warning
amber

Critical / Trial Terminated / Do Not Contact
red

Informational
purple or blue-violet

Maintain accessible contrast ratios.

Typography should be a major part of the identity.

Use a sophisticated sans-serif UI font with excellent readability.

Use large, confident display typography selectively for major headings, inspired by premium contemporary biotech websites.

# 144. AI COPILOT DESIGN

Do not make the whole application a chatbot.

AI is embedded throughout the workflow.

There CAN be a Copilot panel for natural-language requests, but the core product remains structured.

When opened, Copilot should slide in from the right rather than replacing the application's context.

The assistant should understand whatever object the user is viewing.

Example:

User is viewing Novartis.

Ask AI:

“What changed since our last meeting?”

“Who should we contact?”

“Summarize their prostate portfolio.”

“Draft an email about this signal.”

The AI panel should preserve the underlying page context.

# 158. DESIGN NORTH STAR

When making a visual decision, ask:

Would this look credible if displayed on a large screen during a meeting with a VP of Business Development at a top-20 pharmaceutical company?

Would someone working in oncology immediately understand the information hierarchy?

Does the UI reduce cognitive load rather than adding decoration?

Does this feel like a $50,000–$250,000/year enterprise intelligence product?

If not, redesign it.

The final interface should feel:

LESS like an AI wrapper.

MORE like an entirely new category of purpose-built biopharma commercial software.

The user should almost forget that AI is underneath it.

AI should manifest as:

better prioritization
better intelligence
better recommendations
better outreach
less manual work

—not as chat bubbles everywhere.
